### 1. Crossing the Canyon of "Random Hallucination"

In the years since the explosion of artificial intelligence, humanity's quest to let machines program autonomously has gone through three waves:
1. **First wave (the era of completion)**: inline suggestions exemplified by Copilot and Tab autocompletion. Human engineers still bear 99% of system control and error-bailout responsibility;
2. **Second wave (the era of prompt stacking)**: chat-based programming exemplified by open-source agent frameworks. Although it introduced tool calling and multi-step loops, it repeatedly collapses when facing million-line large production projects, mired in the quagmire of "discrete prompting" and "reward hacking";
3. **Third wave (the era of cybernetics and physical anchoring)**: precisely the **Industrial Agentic Cybernetics** defined in this book.

This book's core contribution, established through cross-validation of controlled empirical evidence and formal mechanized checking, is that: **the inherent uncertainty and probabilistic hallucination of large language models can never be eliminated by yet another set of longer prompts; one must introduce classical systems cybernetics, distributed causal ordering, the low-level physical constraints of the compiler, and least-privilege projection, building an unbreakable exoskeleton from a deterministic physical system.**
